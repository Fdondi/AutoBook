$$\overrightarrow{a} \times \overrightarrow{b} = \|\overrightarrow{a}\| \|\overrightarrow{b}\| \sin\theta \, \overrightarrow{e} \tag{6.8}$$

where, as with the dot product, $\theta$ is the angle between $\overrightarrow{a}$ and $\overrightarrow{b}$, and $\overrightarrow{e}$ is a unit vector[^6] orthogonal to the first two. Its direction is established with the right-hand rule. In 3D space, the components of the resulting vector can be computed with the following formula:

$$\mathbf{a} \times \mathbf{b} = \begin{bmatrix} a_2 b_3 - a_3 b_2 \\ a_3 b_1 - a_1 b_3 \\ a_1 b_2 - a_2 b_1 \end{bmatrix} \tag{6.9}$$

where $\mathbf{a} = [a_1 \ a_2 \ a_3]^T$ and $\mathbf{b} = [b_1 \ b_2 \ b_3]^T$.

> The right-hand rule is used to easily determine the direction of a vector resulting from the cross product of two others. First, you point in the direction of the first vector with your remaining fingers, then curl them to point in the direction of the second vector. According to this rule, the thumb of the right hand will point along the direction of the resulting vector, which is normal to the plane formed by the two initial vectors.

$$\overrightarrow{a} \times \overrightarrow{b} = 1.4142 \cdot 3.1623 \sin(0.4636) \overrightarrow{k} = 2\overrightarrow{k} \tag{6.10}$$

$$\mathbf{a}^{\mathcal{S}} \times \mathbf{b}^{\mathcal{S}} = \begin{bmatrix} 1 \cdot 0 - 0 \cdot 3 \\ 0 \cdot 1 - 1 \cdot 0 \\ 1 \cdot 3 - 1 \cdot 1 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 0 \\ 0 \\ 2 \end{bmatrix} \tag{6.11}$$

where $\overrightarrow{k}$ is the unit vector parallel to the $Z$-axis.
