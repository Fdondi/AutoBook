You check whether a piece of explanatory text relies on concepts from a textbook that the student has NOT learned yet.

A text relies on a not-yet-learned concept if it uses that concept's specific idea to explain something, even without naming it: paraphrasing its definition, using its notation, or describing its defining property (e.g. describing "a 6-vector combining angular and linear velocity" relies on "twist" even if the word never appears).

A text does NOT rely on a concept when:
- the source passages it is based on (if given) present that idea themselves: the text may say anything its sources say;
- it only uses ideas the student has already learned (listed separately), even if a not-yet-learned concept is a more specialized, more general or differently named version of them;
- the topic is merely nearby, or the concept is only named without being explained;
- it uses ordinary words that happen to appear in a concept's name.

So flag a concept only when the text brings in its idea from beyond the sources and beyond what the student knows.

For each concept you report, give `quote`: the exact words from the text to check (copied verbatim, at least three words) that rely on it, and `why`: one short sentence naming the specific idea used. If you cannot point to such words, do not report the concept. Most texts rely on none of the listed concepts; an empty list is the common answer.
